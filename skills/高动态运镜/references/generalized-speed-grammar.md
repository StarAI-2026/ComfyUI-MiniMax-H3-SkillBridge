# Generalized high-speed vocabulary and composition grammar

## Purpose

Use the supplied hoverboard sample as evidence of what high energy looks like, not as a mandatory storyboard. Generate a new physical path, camera pursuit, and payoff for each concept.

## Five-part speed evidence model

Every high-speed shot must make these visible:

1. **Subject displacement:** movement across at least two axes or one axis plus strong rotation/banking.
2. **Camera energy:** a fast change of distance, height, side, angle, or orientation—not only passive centering.
3. **Parallax/occlusion:** foreground structures or particles cross the lens while the deep background moves at a different rate.
4. **Physical interaction:** the subject threads, clears, scrapes, clips, vaults, overtakes, lands on, rebounds from, or redirects around something.
5. **Causal reaction:** thrust, gravity, airflow, impact, or friction produces body compression, hair/cloth lag, sparks, spray, debris, wake, reflection shifts, or camera shake.

Also require one substantial apparent-scale or perspective change: the subject fills the frame, pulls away, crosses close to lens, or rapidly changes camera distance.

## Motion archetype library

Choose one primary architecture per case; remix beats only when the physics connect:

- **Urban weave:** closing chase → hard alternating banks through gates → near-miss exit.
- **Vertical slingshot:** steep dive → corkscrew around a target → thrust reversal into a climb.
- **Rail transfer:** grind/contact → spark-loaded gap transfer → wall ride or receiving-rail lock.
- **Overtake:** pursue a vehicle → draft and side swap → explosive pass close to lens.
- **Ground-to-air:** skim/ride → launch and readable air maneuver → landing, drift, or continued flight.
- **Aerial pursuit:** chase a moving target → dodge/intercept → redirect into a new corridor.
- **Ricochet escape:** falling or forward rush → compressed surface contact → rebound into lateral escape.
- **Wave/canyon run:** skim a deforming surface → carve through an arch → climb or burst through spray.

Do not force a landing into an airborne escape, a launch into a rail transfer, or a character gesture into a payoff that should remain spatial.

## Camera module library

Compose two or three sequential camera states. Use bracketed official commands where available and natural English for arcs, rolls, and fly-bys.

- **Rear chase compression:** `[Tracking shot,Push in]`; useful for closing distance or entering a corridor.
- **Lateral whip:** `[Tracking shot,Truck left]` or `[Tracking shot,Truck right]`; useful for banks, overtakes, and rail transfers.
- **Dive pursuit:** `[Tracking shot,Tilt down]` or `[Tracking shot,Pedestal down]`; useful for vertical descent.
- **Rising pursuit:** `[Tracking shot,Tilt up]` or `[Tracking shot,Pedestal up]`; useful for climbs and takeoffs.
- **Impact response:** `[Tracking shot,Shake]`; use briefly at contact, sonic burst, or near miss.
- **Scale release:** `[Tracking shot,Pull out]`; useful when the subject accelerates away or the environment opens up.
- **Close-pass reversal:** push in toward a closing subject, describe the lens pass naturally, then use pull out or truck to reacquire from the opposite side.
- **Arc/orbit/roll:** describe the precise angle, speed, direction, and subject relationship in natural English; do not invent unsupported bracket commands.

Avoid using the same camera trio for every case.

## Flexible timing

- **About 5 seconds:** 0.0-0.5 immediate kinetic lock; 0.5-3.8 one compound traversal with a redirect or obstacle; 3.8-end one decisive spatial payoff. Adjust boundaries to the action.
- **6-8 seconds:** use 3-4 beats and allow one extra reversal, obstacle, or environmental reveal.
- **About 10 seconds:** use 4-5 beats with one large speed contrast and one environmental transition.

These are pacing ranges, not fixed timestamps. Keep actions temporally achievable.

## High-speed lexicon

### Force and acceleration

`explodes forward`, `rockets`, `catapults`, `slingshots`, `surges`, `bursts`, `snaps into acceleration`, `thrust spikes`, `drafts behind`, `brakes against airflow`.

### Direction and body response

`banks hard`, `dives`, `pitches upward`, `yaws`, `corkscrews`, `redirects thrust`, `compresses on contact`, `recoils`, `re-centers under load`, `limbs trail then snap into alignment`.

### Contact and traversal

`threads the gate`, `skims the surface`, `scrapes the rail`, `clips the edge`, `vaults the gap`, `wall-rides`, `ricochets`, `overtakes`, `cuts beneath`, `punches through spray`.

### Camera pursuit

`dives after`, `whips alongside`, `sweeps beneath`, `races past`, `falls behind`, `flips to the opposite side`, `arcs rapidly around`, `reacquires after the close pass`, `rolls with the bank`.

### Parallax and scale

`foreground beams slash across frame`, `cables lash past the lens`, `tower faces expand outward`, `road markings smear beneath`, `the ground spins below`, `the subject fills the frame`, `rapidly shrinks into the corridor`, `the skyline falls away`.

### Environmental reaction

`spray walls erupt`, `sparks fan backward`, `dust curls into a vortex`, `rain peels into ribbons`, `a vapor cone tightens`, `shock rings expand`, `glass reflections whip across armor`, `debris tumbles in the wake`.

## Anti-low-motion rules

Rewrite when the clip relies on any of these as its main event:

- slow push-in plus blinking;
- uniform parallel tracking with constant subject scale;
- hair and cloth motion without large subject travel;
- a static pose framed by particles;
- a final wink or hand gesture with no spatial payoff;
- three camera terms attached to one unchanged path;
- the same launch-orbit-land structure repeated across unrelated concepts.
